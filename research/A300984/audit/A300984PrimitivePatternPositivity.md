# Prime density of finite A300984 patterns with prescribed A082539 status

Let $P$ consist of primes $p$ with no smaller prime $q$ satisfying $q+1\mid p+1$, as in A082539. Let $E$ consist of primes $p\equiv1\pmod {12}$ for which $p+1$ is squarefree. For fixed distinct integers $i\ge2$, prescribe which $2^ip^2$ are members of A300984. Among primes in $E\cap P$, the pattern occurs with positive density relative to all primes. It also occurs with positive prime density in $E\setminus P$. For the single position $i=2$, between 84.12% and 87.03% of $E\cap P$ have $4p^2\in A300984$, in relative natural density.

## Independent local coordinates

Give each reduced residue coordinate $U_r\in\mathbb Z_r^\times$ its uniform probability measure, independently over primes $r$. Write $V_r=v_r(U_r+1)$. The prime number theorem for arithmetic progressions and the Chinese remainder theorem identify the density of each finite congruence event with its product probability. Write $P_z$ for avoidance of all $q+1\mid U+1$ for prime $q\le z$. When evaluated at primes the finitely many $p\le z$ can be discarded. [The signed tail estimate](A300984MajorityLimit.md) adapts Luca, Pizarro-Madariaga and Pomerance's Theorem 3 and gives a prime-density error $O((\log z)^{-c})$ for an obstruction with $z<q<p$, for some $c>0$. This also bounds the measure of the union of all coordinate-model obstructions $q>z$, by first taking a finite union with $z<q\le w$, then sending $w\to\infty$. Denote avoidance of all obstructions in this model by $P_\infty$. The diagonal $q=p$ is excluded in the prime counts throughout. The quadratic and linear square-divisor tails in [the finite-pattern proof](A300984FinitePatterns.md) identify intersections involving these squarefreeness conditions with their model probabilities as well.

Avoidance of any collection of obstructions $q+1\mid U+1$ is a decreasing event in the independent ordered coordinates $V_r$. Two decreasing events have intersection probability at least the product of their probabilities by Harris's inequality. For finite coordinate systems this follows by induction, using the nonnegative covariance of two monotone functions of one random variable at each step. Infinite versions used below follow by continuity for decreasing sequences of finite events.

Take $z$ such that the tail obstruction event has measure less than $1/2$. The probability of $P_z$ is positive, since one can require $U_2\equiv1\pmod4$ and $U_r\equiv1\pmod r$ for all odd $r\le z+1$. In this cylinder $U+1$ has one factor of two and no odd divisor from those primes, while every $q+1\ge3$, $q\le z$, needs at least one of these forbidden factors. Harris then shows that $P_\infty$ has positive probability.

Eligibility $E$ is the decreasing event $V_2=1,V_3=0,V_r\le1$ at all $r\ge5$, since $V_2\ge1$ for every 2-adic unit. It has positive measure by convergence of the product with factors $1-1/[r(r-1)]$. Any finite number of additional constraints $V_r=0$ at odd primes can be imposed at positive probability. Harris therefore gives positive probability to their intersection with $E\cap P_\infty$.

## Adding the pattern

Put $F_i(x)=2^{i+1}(x^2+x+1)-(x+2)^2$. For $p\in E$, membership of $2^ip^2$ is equivalent to squarefreeness of $F_i(p)$. Write $I$ for present and $J$ for absent positions. For $j\in J$, choose a distinct prime with a nonzero simple root of $F_j$ different from $-1$ and avoiding every $F_i$, $i\in I$, as in [the finite-pattern proof](A300984FinitePatterns.md). At each such prime reserve the root's lift modulo its square.

Put into a finite set $B$ all odd reserved primes, all odd primes up to $2|I|+7$, and all prime factors of the leading coefficients, discriminants and the nonzero $F_i(-1)=2^{i+1}-1$ for $i\in I$. Omit 2 from $B$. Require $V_r=0$ at all $r\in B$. By the preceding argument the resulting event $H\subset E\cap P_\infty$ has positive measure. Conditional on the valuations, unit residues at the different primes are independent with their local conditional laws. At the reserved primes take the reserved lift, and at the other primes of $B$ take $U_r\equiv-2\pmod{r^2}$. All these cylinders have fixed positive probability given $V_r=0$. For $r=3$ this choice has $F_i(-2)=3\cdot2^{i+1}$ divisible by exactly one factor of 3. Every $F_i$ is odd on odd $p$.

For $r\ge5$ outside $B$, if $V_r\ge1$ then $F_i(U_r)\equiv F_i(-1)\ne0\pmod r$ for every $i\in I$. If $V_r=0$, then $r(r-2)$ possible residue classes modulo $r^2$ are equally likely. At most $2|I|$ of them fail a required squarefree test by simple-root lifting. The product of $1-2|I|/[r(r-2)]$ over those primes converges to a positive number. Together with the finite local factors it gives a uniform strictly positive lower bound conditional on every allowed valuation vector. This proves positive density for the pattern with $p\in E\cap P$. Existence of the full pattern density, as opposed to a positive lower bound, follows by approximating the proper obstructions and square divisors with finite congruence tests. Negating a finite number of squarefreeness indicators leaves the approximation valid.

For a positive-density set in $E\setminus P$, take one prime $q\equiv1\pmod {4\prod_{r\in B}r}$ with $q+1$ squarefree. Such primes have positive density by the linear square-divisor sieve in that progression. The odd squarefree integer $d=(q+1)/2$ is coprime to all the bad and reserved primes. Require $U_r\equiv-1+r\pmod{r^2}$ for each $r\mid d$. These finitely many congruences force $q+1\mid U+1$, preserve squarefreeness of $U+1$, and make all the present $F_i$ nonzero modulo these primes. Away from them the preceding local construction applies without imposing $P_\infty$. All prime inputs $p>q$ constructed in this way are nonprimitive.

## An explicit bound for the first exponent

Here $F_2(x)=7x^2+4x+4$. It is odd on odd arguments. Its discriminant $-96$ has exactly one factor of 3, so it has no root modulo 9. At 7 its sole root modulo 7 is $-1$. Writing $x=-1+7t$ gives $F_2(x)=7-70t+343t^2$. Thus exactly one of the six unit values of $t\pmod7$ fails when $V_7=1$, and every value passes when $V_7=0$ or $V_7\ge2$. For odd primes $r\ge5, r\ne7$, at most two classes fail among the $r(r-2)$ classes with $V_r=0$. Every class with $V_r\ge1$ passes, since $F_2(-1)=7$. Therefore the squarefree survival probability conditional on any valuation vector is at least

$$
\frac56\prod_{r\ge5,\,r\text{ prime},\,r\ne7}\left(1-\frac2{r(r-2)}\right)
\ \ge\ \frac56\left(1-\sum_{n\ge5,\,n\text{ odd},\,n\ne7}\frac2{n(n-2)}\right)=\frac{38}{63}.
$$

The series telescopes using $2/[n(n-2)]=1/(n-2)-1/n$. On $P_\infty$, the obstruction $q=13$ forces $V_7=0$ because $13+1=14$ and $V_2\ge1$. The local factor at 7 is therefore exactly one on this event. Removing the factor $5/6$ and retaining the exact factors through 10,000 improves the rigorous lower bound to $0.841206209$. For $r\ne2,3,7$, the number of roots is two or zero according as $-96$ is or is not a square modulo $r$. Every root is a unit different from $-1$ and lifts uniquely modulo $r^2$. The remaining product is at least $1-1/9999$ by the same telescoping sum. The [exact rational checker](check_a300984_primitive_first_exponent.py) verifies 610 contributing primes and the bound. Integrating over the positive-measure event $E\cap P_\infty$ gives the stated ratio. In particular the existing limit for the proportion of A082539 primes among distinct represented primes of A300984 is strictly positive, since $4p^2\le Y$ for every $p\le\sqrt Y/2$. The 84.12% bound concerns the conditional first-exponent probability among eligible primitive primes. The A300984 majority uses a different denominator and remains open.

For an upper bound, first condition the coordinate model on $E$ and on $V_r=0$ at each prime $r\le10000$ for which $2r-1$ is prime, apart from $r=2,3$. These latter conditions are forced by $P_\infty$ and include $r=7$. Independence of the coordinates is preserved by this prime-by-prime conditioning. For the remaining $r\ge5$, the conditional probability of passing $r^2\nmid F_2(U)$ is nondecreasing in $V_r$. It is one at $V_r\ge1$ and at most one at $V_r=0$. The product of the local survival probabilities up to 10,000 is therefore increasing. Harris's inequality applied to its negative and the decreasing indicator of $P_\infty$ shows that further conditioning on $P_\infty$ can only lower its expectation. At a split prime $r$ forced to $V_r=0$, the expected factor is $1-2/[r(r-2)]$. At a free split prime it is $1-2/[r(r-1)-1]$, since eligibility removes exactly one unit class modulo $r^2$. All other factors are one. Their finite product is an upper bound even after including the remaining squarefree tests. The checker verifies the outward-rounded interval $[0.841206209,0.870297024]$ with exact rational arithmetic.
