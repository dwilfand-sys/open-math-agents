# A compulsory test from two selected small primes

Fix the small-prime assignment for $M=(p+1)/2$ in the A300984 divisor sieve. Whenever two distinct selected primes $a,b$ both divide $M$, put $c=ab$. If a prime $r$ also divides $M$ and $2cr-1$ is prime, $cr$ supplies an A082539 obstruction apart from the finitely many equality cases for a fixed finite list. The first tables test the 24 previously listed surviving small-prime pairs with smallest products, beginning with $5\cdot13$ and $5\cdot17$. Supplementary tables test the other 60 surviving pairs on three intervals. A pair survives the small sieve when $2ab-1$ is composite. Either of the pair's individual small primes may have the opposite residue modulo 6 from $r$. The test requires $r\equiv c\pmod6$.

In each sign interval retain a prime $r$ only if $2ur-1$ is composite for **every** selected small prime $u\equiv r\pmod6$. For positive high primes also require $2r-1$ composite. For an opposite-residue $u$ the shifted form is already divisible by 3. Thus the retained coordinates were unused by all ordinary one-small-prime tests, including the simultaneous negative-slope mandatory test. Fixing the small assignment determines the guard and leaves the remaining high coordinates independent. The probability that $r\mid M$ is at least $1/r$ in the eligible or first-witness conditional measure of the earlier certificate.

For each eligible pair $a,b$, the program enumerates the retained $r$ by blocks. A block with $n$ such primes ending at $U$ has absence probability at most $U/(U+n)$ by the product inequality. Block factors multiply with outward rounding. If a mask contains more than one listed pair eligible for the same sign, it may take the smallest of their upper survival factors. This last choice requires only the small assignment.

The placement determines which earlier tests have overlapping coordinates.

- For a small mask selecting exactly two listed primes $a,b$, the billion-divisor pair graph first deletes all vertices $r$ for which any one of $2r-1,2ar-1,2br-1,2abr-1$ is prime. Thus a composite-slope neighbor cannot be a vertex of that graph. The [prior-bound replay](check_a300984_low_pair_prior.cjs) checks that this graph supplies the actual bound chosen by the original composer in every one of the 24 exact pair masks, in both the eligibility and first-witness measures. In the interval $(50\,000,400\,000]$ use only these exact masks and require $abr>20$ million. The remaining initial single-outside-coordinate exclusions can only come from $1,a,b$ and the guard removes them. Every conditioned medium center is at most $50$ thousand, and every high-star leaf exceeds $4$ million. The new factor therefore multiplies the final bound directly. This conclusion depends on the center cutoff.
- In $(400\,000,4\,000\,000]$ this direct multiplication applies to every mask with a listed pair. For exactly two use the argument above. A mask with three or more small primes uses only the original nonlinear graph with divisor cutoff $20$ million. Every edge needs two free outer vertices above $73$, so even its largest vertex is below $20\,000\,000/73<400\,000$. For a single remaining outside coordinate, a product of two distinct small primes is at least $5\cdot11$, which already times $400$ thousand exceeds $20$ million. Only the filtered singleton and one-small-prime factors remain.
- Short tables on $(4\,000\,000,14\,000\,000]$ and $(14\,000\,000,100\,000\,000]$ give guaranteed tests after the old graph, by the preceding argument for the applicable small masks. The two intervals have disjoint high coordinates. Their combined short mandatory factor applies even if it overlaps the high leaves of a layered star.
- A table on $(100\,000\,000,10^9]$ gives another guaranteed test. Its coordinates are disjoint from the earlier long mandatory linear test by the guard. Multiply the two long survival factors with an upper round. Any available medium row supported at most at $100$ million can multiply its survival factor by this combined long factor. The always-available row can multiply it by the short composite factor. A broad medium row that overlaps both intervals retains its own bound.
- A table on $(3\cdot10^9,4\cdot10^9]$ can multiply the full survivor estimate directly when earlier medium-center tables end at $3\cdot10^9$. The small linear tests and positive singletons still active there are removed by the guard. Two disjoint such intervals multiply, as do different leaf signs.

The choice of the old graph matters here. For an exact pair mask the reference driver computes both the old two-outer bound and $\lceil wD_{ab}\rceil$, where $w$ is that mask's mass after its initial fixed-coordinate exclusions and $D_{ab}$ is its billion-divisor pair graph bound. The replay reconstructs both bounds with the actual mask, its actual previously forbidden coordinates, and the appropriate local weights. It checks $\lceil wD_{ab}\rceil$ is no larger in all 48 cases (24 pairs in each of two measures). Thus one can use the pair graph alone to derive exactly the number supplied to every downstream correction. When a medium gate is closed, conditioning may expose those short high coordinates actually used by the old graph. The compulsory composite coordinates are absent from that graph by the vertex deletion or the smaller divisor cutoff, so they remain free. This also justifies the always-on short factor on the closed gate.

For the other 60 legal exact pairs, the billion-divisor pair table contains no row, and the earlier billion-divisor single table applies at most to one selected prime. Their old nonlinear bound is therefore that of the $20$-million two-outer graph. The replay verifies this identity for all 60 remaining masks in both measures and finds its largest vertex is $240{,}953$. The supplementary tables cover $(400\,000,4\,000\,000]$, $(4\,000\,000,14\,000\,000]$ and $(14\,000\,000,100\,000\,000]$. All use the same arguments as the corresponding original table. Within each same-sign interval, `merge` takes the best single applicable pair from the union of the two pair lists. No independence between two pair rows with overlapping high intervals is needed.

[CunninghamCompositeSlopes.cs](CunninghamCompositeSlopes.cs) accepts a sign (5 or 1), open lower endpoint and closed upper endpoint. Each output row gives $a,b$, the selected same-sign guard mask, pair count and upper survival factor in units $10^{-15}$. The positive guard order is $13,43,61,67,73$, and the negative order is $5,11,17,23,29,41,47,53,59,71$. Only guard masks consistent with $a,b$ are printed. [lagneau_composite_slopes.cjs](lagneau_composite_slopes.cjs) reads the tables, checks their shapes and supplies mandatory or independent-coordinate callbacks. [check_a300984_composite_slopes.cjs](check_a300984_composite_slopes.cjs) replays each table using a different sieve and direct subset counts. The low-pair prior check takes the same original graph directory as the composition driver, using `node check_a300984_low_pair_prior.cjs 20000000 73 directory`.

For the mask selecting exactly $5,13$, the $(3\cdot10^9,4\cdot10^9]$ interval contains 2,816,856 newly usable negative prime coordinates. Their survival factor is at most $0.999189752468443$. For exactly $5,17$ there are 2,724,886 positive coordinates with survival at most $0.999216204490077$.

Across the six intervals and both signs the second implementation reproduced every one of the 39,168 counts and every rounded survival factor. The [compulsory-interval test](check_a300984_composite_mandatory.py) enumerates 128 exact twelve-coordinate product models and checks both the conditional and integrated survival inequalities. The model allows shared broad and short leaves, two distinct long mandatory components, an additional long extension, and an earlier event that can use a short high coordinate when its gate is closed.

Pass `remaining` as the fourth generator argument for the supplementary data. In each of the three intervals the negative table has 19,456 cells and the positive table 560. The second program reproduced all 60,048 supplementary counts and upper factors. The two sets of certificates contain 99,216 count-and-factor cells in total.
