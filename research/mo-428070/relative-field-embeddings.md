# The largest possible field in a finite group's augmentation algebra

Let \(F\) be a field of characteristic zero, \(G\ne1\) a finite group of order \(N\), and work in one algebraic closure of \(F\). For each prime \(p\mid N\) choose a primitive \(p\)-th root of unity and set
\[
 E=\bigcap_{p\mid N}F(\zeta_p),\qquad
 e_G=N^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g,\qquad I_F(G)=(1-e_G)F[G].
\]
A finite extension \(K/F\) admits a unital \(F\)-algebra embedding into \(I_F(G)\) if and only if it is \(F\)-isomorphic to a subfield of \(E\). Every such extension also admits an embedding into the center of \(I_F(G)\). The common character field has the explicit description
\[
 \bigcap_{\chi\in\mathrm{Irr}(G),\ \chi\ne 1_G}F(\chi)=E,
\]
where \(F(\chi)\) is generated by all the values of the ordinary irreducible character \(\chi\). These are assertions about the existence of an embedding. An arbitrarily chosen noncentral embedding may have noncentral image.

## Necessity from the eigenspaces of a translation

If \(K\) embeds in the augmentation algebra, right multiplication makes \(I_F(G)\) a \(K\)-space. Thus \(d=[K:F]\) divides \(N-1\). Let \(f\) be a primitive element's irreducible polynomial, and let \(T\) be the operator given by this element. For \(p\mid N\), choose \(g\in G\) of order \(p\) and put \(L_p=F(\zeta_p)\). Left multiplication by \(g\) on \(L_p[G]\) is a union of \(N/p\) disjoint \(p\)-cycles on the group basis. Its \(\zeta_p\)-eigenspace has dimension \(N/p\), has augmentation zero, and is stable under the extension of \(T\).

The monic irreducible factors of \(f\) over \(L_p\) have a common degree \(b\), because \(L_p/F\) is Galois and the product over any Galois orbit of factors belongs to \(F[X]\). Therefore \(b\mid d\). All irreducible factors of the characteristic polynomial of the restricted \(T\) divide \(f\), which annihilates it. Thus \(b\mid N/p\). Since \(d\) divides \(N-1\), this forces \(b=1\). All the roots of the same \(f\) lie in every \(L_p\). Taking one root realizes \(K\) simultaneously inside their intersection.

## Sufficiency by the action on the character table

Suppose \(F\subseteq K\subseteq E\), and put \(L=F(\zeta_{\exp G})\). Each \(L_p/F\) is cyclic of degree dividing \(p-1\), so \(K/F\) is cyclic and its degree \(d\) is coprime to \(N\). Let \(\Gamma=\mathrm{Gal}(L/F)\). Write \(\sigma(\zeta_{\exp G})=\zeta_{\exp G}^{\,a}\). If \(\sigma\) acts nontrivially on \(K\), the order \(h>1\) of its restriction divides \(d\).

Suppose \(g\ne1\) and \(g^a\) is conjugate to \(g\). A conjugating element normalizes \(\langle g\rangle\) and induces the automorphism given by \(a\). Hence for each \(p\mid |g|\) the multiplicative order of \(a\bmod p\) divides the order of a group element, and thus divides \(N\). That same order is the order of \(\sigma\) on \(L_p\); its restriction to \(K\) has order \(h\), which therefore divides \(N\). This contradicts \(\gcd(d,N)=1\). Exactly the identity conjugacy class is fixed by the power map.

In the character table the identity \(\sigma(\chi(g))=\chi(g^a)\) identifies the row and column permutation matrices up to similarity, since the character table is invertible. They have the same trace. Thus exactly one irreducible character is fixed by \(\sigma\), and it is the trivial character. In particular every stabilizer of a nonprincipal irreducible character in \(\Gamma\) fixes \(K\) pointwise. This is Brauer's permutation lemma applied to a cyclic action. The ordinary character values can be taken in the cyclotomic field inside the algebraic closure of \(F\). [Richard Brauer, 1941](https://doi.org/10.2307/1968774) · [I. M. Isaacs, Lemma 1.1 and the preceding Galois action](https://msp.org/pjm/1967/23-2/pjm-v23-n2-p06-s.pdf)

For an explicit central embedding, let
\[
 e_\chi=\frac{\chi(1)}{N}\sum_{g\in G}\chi(g^{-1})g.
\]
These orthogonal central idempotents in \(L[G]\) sum to 1, with \(e_{1_G}=e_G\). Choose one character per nonprincipal \(\Gamma\)-orbit and, for \(b\in K\), put
\[
 \iota(b)=\sum_{\chi\ {\rm chosen}}\;\sum_{\sigma\in\Gamma/\mathrm{Stab}(\chi)}
          \sigma(b)e_{\sigma\chi}.
\]
The stabilizer statement makes the expression independent of coset representatives. Galois invariance puts its coefficients in \(F\); orthogonality gives multiplicativity and \(\iota(1)=1-e_G\ne0\). This proves the central embedding. It also shows \(E\subseteq F(\chi)\) for every nonprincipal character. Conversely any subfield of the intersection of those character fields admits the same central construction, because every stabilizer fixes every one of its elements. Taking that whole intersection and applying necessity proves equality: its primitive polynomial splits in every \(L_p\), including its originally chosen root. The usual description of centers of Wedderburn components gives the same construction. [Wedderga, §9.4, *Characters and primitive central idempotents*](https://docs.gap-system.org/pkg/wedderga/doc/chap9_mj.html)

## Two explicit roots over quadratic base fields

* Over \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{10})\), take \(G=C_5=\langle g\rangle\). The element \(t=g+g^4-g^2-g^3\) satisfies \(t^2=5(1-e_G)\). Therefore \((\sqrt{10}/5)t\) squares to \(2(1-e_G)\), even though \(2\) is a nonsquare in \(F\). Here \(F(\sqrt2)=F(\sqrt5)\) is contained in \(F(\zeta_5)\).
* For the nonabelian group of order 21 write \(a^7=b^3=1,\ bab^{-1}=a^2\). Let \(F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{21})\), \(e_7=7^{-1}\sum_{i=0}^6a^i\), and \(t=a+a^2+a^4-a^3-a^5-a^6\). Multiplication by 2 preserves the quadratic residues modulo 7, so \(t\) is central. The other summand \(e_7(b-b^2)\) is central in the quotient corner. The central element
  \[
   x=(\sqrt{21}/7)t+e_7(b-b^2)
  \]
  satisfies \(x^2=-3(1-e_G)\). In fact \(t^2=-7(1-e_7)\) and the second summand squares to \(-3(e_7-e_G)\). Here \(F(\sqrt{-3})=F(\sqrt{-7})\) lies in both required cyclotomic fields. Exact group-basis multiplication of \(7x\) gave coefficient \(-140\) at the identity and \(7\) at every other element of its square. For the \(C_5\) example the square of the fivefold multiple has coefficient \(40\) at the identity and \(-10\) elsewhere.
