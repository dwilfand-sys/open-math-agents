# A trace obstruction for the rational augmentation representation

Hugo MTV's second question asks whether the augmentation ideal \(I\) of a nontrivial finite group \(G\) can contain a square root of twice its identity \(u\). The public thread has a comment by JP McCarthy addressing the whole group algebra, followed by the author's explicit request about \(I\). The source viewed for this note displays no answer to the latter. [MathOverflow question and comments](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/428070/existence-of-sqrt2-in-a-finite-group-algebra-over-mathbbq)

Suppose \(x^2=2u\). Any solution in the whole algebra has augmentation zero, since the square of its rational augmentation is zero. Right multiplication by \(x\) makes \(I\) a vector space over \(K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), with \(\sqrt2\) acting as this multiplication. Left translation by \(g\ne1\) is \(K\)-linear and has finite order. Its \(K\)-trace \(s\) is therefore a sum of roots of unity and belongs to the ring of integers \(\mathcal O_K=\mathbb Z[\sqrt2]\). Write \(s=a+b\sqrt2\) with \(a,b\in\mathbb Z\). Its rational trace on \(I\) equals \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(s)=2a\). Indeed each entry \(v+w\sqrt2\) of a \(K\)-matrix becomes the rational block \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}v&2w\\ w&v\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). On the other hand the permutation of the basis \(G\) by \(g\) has no fixed point and thus has trace zero on \(\mathbb Q[G]\). Its action on the one-dimensional quotient \(\mathbb Q[G]/I\) is trivial. Its trace on \(I\) is consequently \(-1\), a contradiction. The trivial group has the solution \(x=u=0\).

The standard ingredients have precise references. Emmanuel Kowalski proves the integrality of finite-group character values in *An Introduction to the Representation Theory of Groups*, Corollary A.1.4. Brian Conrad computes the ring of integers of each quadratic field in *Quadratic integer rings*, Theorem 1.1. Together they give the parity obstruction above. More generally, if a rational \(G\)-representation is obtained by restricting scalars from a number field \(K\), all its character values lie in \(\operatorname{Tr}_{K/\mathbb Q}(\mathcal O_K)\). Thus the augmentation character forces that trace ideal to be all of \(\mathbb Z\), since it contains \(-1\). [Kowalski, Corollary A.1.4](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~kowalski/representation-theory.pdf) · [Conrad, Theorem 1.1](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/210BPage/handouts/quadint.pdf)

A finite-group action over a number field preserves the sum of the translates of any full \(\mathcal O_K\)-lattice. This follows by taking the finite sum of its translates, which contains the starting lattice, is finitely generated and is fixed by the group. Serre's *Three letters to Walter Feit on group representations and quaternions* studies the further question of whether a free invariant lattice exists in specific representations. The trace argument only uses integrality of the eigenvalues. The sources examined establish these classical ingredients. Historical priority for applying them to this precise question remains undetermined. [Serre, published correspondence](https://www.college-de-france.fr/media/jean-pierre-serre/UPL2511561210740026668_8___Three_letters_to_Walter_Feit.pdf)
