# Number fields in a rational augmentation algebra

Let \(G\) be a nontrivial finite group of order \(N\), let \(e_G=N^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g\), and write \(I_G=(1-e_G)\mathbb Q[G]\). Its identity is \(u_G=1-e_G\). Let \(K/\mathbb Q\) be a number field of degree \(d>1\).

**Theorem.** The following conditions are equivalent.

1. There is a unital \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra embedding \(K\hookrightarrow I_G\).
2. There is a unital \(\mathbb Q\)-algebra embedding \(K\hookrightarrow Z(I_G)\).
3. For some odd prime \(p\), \(G\) is a \(p\)-group and \(K\) is isomorphic over \(\mathbb Q\) to a subfield of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\).

The image of the embedding in (2) is central in the whole group algebra. All the fields in (3) are cyclic Galois extensions of \(\mathbb Q\). The degree-one field embeds via \(a\mapsto a u_G\) for every nontrivial group. For \(G=1\) the augmentation algebra is zero and contains no field.

## Necessity

Take an embedding as in (1). Right multiplication by its image makes \(I_G\) a vector space over \(K\). Thus \(d\mid N-1\), so \(\gcd(d,N)=1\). Choose a primitive element of \(K/\mathbb Q\), write \(f\) for its monic irreducible polynomial, and \(T\) for right multiplication by its image on \(I_G\). Then \(f(T)=0\).

For each \(p\mid N\), take \(g\in G\) of order \(p\), set \(L=\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\), and consider left translation by \(g\) on \(L[G]\). Its orbits on the basis are the \(N/p\) sets \(\langle g\rangle b\), each a \(p\)-cycle. On each cycle the \(\zeta_p\)-eigenspace has dimension one. Hence the full eigenspace \(V\) has \(L\)-dimension \(N/p\). It lies in \(L\otimes_{\mathbb Q} I_G\), since augmentation is invariant under translation whereas \(\zeta_p\ne1\). Left translation commutes with \(T\), so \(V\) is preserved by the scalar extension of \(T\).

The irreducible factors of \(f\) over \(L\) have a common degree \(b\). Indeed \(\operatorname{Gal}(L/\mathbb Q)\) permutes the monic factors, and the product over each orbit would be a rational divisor of the irreducible \(f\). Thus \(b\mid d\). The characteristic polynomial of \(T|_V\) has all its irreducible factors among those of \(f\), since \(f\) annihilates \(T|_V\). Hence \(b\mid N/p\). Coprimality gives \(b=1\). The splitting field of \(f\) is therefore contained in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) for every \(p\mid N\).

For \(p=2\) this cyclotomic field is \(\mathbb Q\). For distinct primes \(p,q\), the intersection of their cyclotomic fields is also \(\mathbb Q\). Indeed their compositum is \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{pq})\), whose degree \((p-1)(q-1)\) is the product of the two degrees. Since \(d>1\), exactly one prime divides \(N\), it is odd, and (3) follows.

## Central construction

Fix an identification of \(K\) with a subfield of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\). In a nontrivial finite \(p\)-group choose a central subgroup \(H=\langle h\rangle\) of order \(p\) and form \(e_H=p^{-1}\sum_{h'\in H}h'\). The two orthogonal central idempotents \(1-e_H\) and \(e_H-e_G\) sum to \(u_G\). Evaluation \(h\mapsto\zeta_p\) identifies the first subalgebra \((1-e_H)\mathbb Q[H]\) with \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\), with identity \(1-e_H\). Its image inside \(\mathbb Q[G]\) is central because \(H\) is central. [G. Eric Moorhouse, *Cyclotomic Fields*, §7, equation (7.1)](https://www.ericmoorhouse.org/handouts/cyclotomic_fields.pdf)

If \(G=H\), this already gives (2). Otherwise, the map \(\bar g\mapsto e_Hg\) identifies \(\mathbb Q[G/H]\) with the direct summand \(e_H\mathbb Q[G]\), and takes \(e_{G/H}\) to \(e_G\). By induction, its augmentation subalgebra contains a central copy of \(K\) with identity \(e_H-e_G\). This copy is central in \(\mathbb Q[G]\), since the other direct summands annihilate it. Adding the two embeddings gives a homomorphism on account of orthogonality. Its identity is \(u_G\), and projection to the first block proves injectivity. This proves (2), which immediately gives (1).

For a rational nonsquare \(c\), an element \(x\) with \(x^2=c u_G\) generates such a copy of \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt c)\). The unique quadratic subfield of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt{(-1)^{(p-1)/2}p})\), recovering the square-class criterion. The companion note treats \(c=2\) directly by trace parity. [Trace argument and sources](mo428070-trace-and-sources.md)

## Relation to published work

The criterion also follows by assembling established character-theoretic results. For necessity start as above with \(d\mid N-1\). Let \(\chi\) be any nonprincipal complex irreducible character with representation \(\rho\), let \(E=\mathbb Q(\chi)\), and let \(a\in I_G\) represent the primitive element. Since the trivial averaging projector acts as zero in \(\rho\), \(u_G\) acts as the identity and \(f(\rho(a))=0\). Each trace \(\operatorname{tr}(\rho(a)^j)=\chi(a^j)\) lies in \(E\) by linear extension. Newton's identities then put the characteristic polynomial in \(E[X]\). The field \(E\) is a subfield of an abelian cyclotomic extension and is Galois over \(\mathbb Q\). The equal-degree argument above now gives a common factor degree dividing both \(d\) and \(\chi(1)\). The classical divisibility \(\chi(1)\mid N\) forces this factor degree to equal one. Thus the splitting field of \(f\) lies in every nonprincipal character field, including when the original embedding was noncentral. [E. Kowalski, *An Introduction to the Representation Theory of Groups*, §4.7](https://people.math.ethz.ch/~kowalski/representation-theory.pdf)

Navarro and Tiep proved that for every prime \(p\mid N\) there is a nonprincipal character with values in \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) and degree prime to \(p\). This yields exactly the cyclotomic restriction in the necessity argument. The published introduction of Giannelli, Hung, Schaeffer Fry and Vallejo identifies the 2006 source, and its Theorem A strengthens the conclusion to a character of degree coprime to both \(2\) and \(p\). [Navarro and Tiep, *Characters of p′-degree with cyclotomic field of values*, Proc. AMS 134 (2006), 2833–2837](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-06-08564-9) · [Giannelli et al., *Characters of π′-degree and small cyclotomic fields*, introduction and Theorem A](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10231-020-01025-x)

For sufficiency, Barker identifies the center of the rational Wedderburn component with the vertex field in §2. His Theorem 5.13 identifies every nontrivial such field for an odd \(p\)-group with \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_{p^j})\) for \(j\ge1\). Choosing the standard embedding of \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\) in each factor and taking the diagonal gives the required central embedding. The familiar faithful-character argument gives the needed inclusion even more directly. Every nonprincipal complex irreducible character descends to a faithful representation of a nontrivial quotient \(p\)-group. A central element of order \(p\) acts there by a scalar of exact order \(p\), so its normalized character value generates \(\mathbb Q(\zeta_p)\). This uses faithfulness on that quotient, including when the original \(p\)-group admits no faithful irreducible representation. [L. Barker, *Genotypes of irreducible representations of finite p-groups*, §2, Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1809.10957) · [G. Henniart and M. Oi, Appendix A.2, Lemma A.4, on the faithful central character](https://hrcak.srce.hr/en/file/453160)

The public source located for the exact augmentation-ideal square-root question is Hugo MTV's 2022 question. The references above provide an independent deduction of the general classification. Searches for unital number fields in the rational augmentation algebra, common subfields of nonprincipal character fields, and the corresponding faithfulness criterion did not establish where the equivalence in precisely this form first appeared. The elementary eigenspace and central-subgroup proof given here was derived during this research. Its historical priority and a Lean verification remain unestablished.
